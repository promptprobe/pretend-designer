# Stripe

- Reference: https://stripe.com/
- Observed: 2026-09-04 at 1440×1000 and 375×812
- Direction: product evidence + modular systems marketing
- Confidence: high for visible layout and computed styles; homepage modules and campaigns change frequently.

## Job and thesis

The homepage helps companies recognize a broad financial platform while proving that the platform can handle concrete payment, billing, issuing, stablecoin, and commerce workflows. Its thesis is financial infrastructure made tangible through real interface states.

## Measured evidence

- Body and hero use `sohne-var` with `SF Pro Display` fallback.
- Desktop hero: about 48px with 55.2px line height, weight 300, and -0.96px tracking over a roughly 959px measure.
- Common sampled radii were 0px, 4px, 5px, and 6px.
- The observed page contained 183 SVGs, five canvases, 42 images, and no video element.
- Dominant UI colors included deep ink around `rgb(6,27,49)`, muted slate, white, Stripe violet around `rgb(83,58,253)`, and product accents including orange and periwinkle.

## Page geometry and visual system

Faint full-height grid rules align the navigation, hero, logo strip, and later modules. A large animated color field occupies the right side of the hero and is cropped by the viewport rather than placed inside a decorative card. The headline begins as a dark concrete claim, then moves into lower-contrast explanatory copy.

Product sections use an asymmetric mosaic of interface demonstrations. The modules are cards, but their purpose is evidential: each contains a payment flow, usage meter, chart, device state, or commerce interaction. Their internal compositions differ while the outer grid remains disciplined.

## Mobile behavior

At 375px, the hero keeps only the essential claim in the first screen, places the graphic behind and above the type, and turns both actions into full-width controls. The logo strip becomes horizontally clipped, and the next explanatory statement enters immediately. The desktop's long multi-sentence headline is edited for mobile rather than merely wrapped.

## Why it works

The brand system is consistent across abstract color, grid, typography, and actual product UI. The colorful field attracts attention, but credibility comes from interface evidence. Fine rules and restrained radii prevent the dense product mosaic from becoming a generic card catalog.

## Transferable rules

- Demonstrate platform breadth through specific interface states, not feature icons.
- Use one outer grid to hold varied internal compositions.
- Let abstract brand motion occupy a structural region rather than a rounded hero banner.
- Edit the mobile message; do not force the full desktop manifesto into the first screen.
- Cards can be valid when each is a meaningful product demonstration and the surrounding geometry is not the standard three-up SaaS pattern.

## Do not copy

Do not reuse Stripe's violet, gradient field, grid proportions, payment UI, customer logos, Söhne typography, or copy. A purple gradient plus white cards is specifically not the transferable lesson.
