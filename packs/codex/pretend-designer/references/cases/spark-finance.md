## Job and thesis

The site explains how institutional capital moves across Spark's savings, credit, borrowing, and liquidity products. Its thesis is a capital-allocation map rendered as a modern financial publication.

## Page geometry and visual system

The hero is a full-viewport field of fine streamlines converging behind an oversized claim. A compact violet announcement strip and white navigation provide an institutional shell. The main CTA is a large outlined square with an arrow, deliberately different from the conventional pill button.

The page later alternates dark product indices, two-column outlined modules, and split-screen explanations. Capital-flow diagrams combine stylized wallet or vault UI with connecting lines and a text explanation. Numbering and progress rails make the long page feel sequenced rather than endlessly stacked.

## Mobile behavior

At 375px, the headline remains large but changes to a compact four-line composition. The streamline graphic survives as a structural background. The CTA stays square, and a live liquidity figure enters directly below the hero, preserving financial proof in the first screen.

## Why it works

The line field is not decoration alone: it represents allocation and routing, which is the product story. The display face and square CTA create identity while Inter remains in a supporting reading role. Product modules use small radii and strong black/white transitions to maintain institutional weight.

## Transferable rules

- Turn the product's central verb into a diagram: allocate, route, connect, settle, or verify.
- Use background lines as information-bearing structure, not generic waves.
- Pair a neutral body face with a distinctive display face and page geometry.
- Introduce financial proof early through a live or clearly sourced figure.
- A two-by-two product index can work when scale, sequencing, and product signals prevent it from reading as generic feature cards.

## Do not copy

Do not reuse Spark's name, star mark, exact violet/blush gradient, streamlines, product icons, KPI values, or interface mockups. The transferable principle is visualizing capital movement, not repeating the brand artwork.
